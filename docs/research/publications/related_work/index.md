## CoBEA: Framework for Evolving Hardware by Direct Manipulation of FPGA Bitstreams (2022)

**Jörn Hoffmann, Clemens Fritzsch, Martin Bogdan** <br>
**Publication:** GECCO '22 Companion — Proceedings of the Genetic and Evolutionary Computation Conference Companion, pp. 112–115, ACM, 2022 <br>

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

- [:fontawesome-solid-file-pdf:{ .lg .middle }

- [:simple-doi:{ .lg .middle }
__DOI Link__
](https://doi.org/10.1145/3520304.3528821)

</div>

???+ abstract
    This paper presents CoBEA, a framework for evolving hardware designs through direct manipulation of FPGA bitstreams. The work represents an independent effort to perform bitstream-level evolution on modern FPGAs, complementing the Bitstream Evolution project's approach. CoBEA provides tools for genetic manipulation of circuit configurations at the bitstream level, enabling intrinsic evolutionary experiments.

??? quote "Suggested Citation"
    Hoffmann, J., Fritzsch, C., & Bogdan, M. (2022). CoBEA: framework for evolving hardware by direct manipulation of FPGA bitstreams. In: *Proceedings of the Genetic and Evolutionary Computation Conference Companion (GECCO '22 Companion)*, pp. 112–115. ACM.

## Evolved Transistor Array Robot Controllers (2020)

**Michael Garvie, Ittai Flascher, Andrew Philippides, Adrian Thompson, Phil Husbands** <br>
**Publication:** Evolutionary Computation, Vol. 28, No. 4, pp. 677–708, MIT Press, 2020 <br>

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

- [:material-file-document-multiple:{ .lg .middle }
__MIT Press Direct__
](https://direct.mit.edu/evco/article/28/4/677/95087/Evolved-Transistor-Array-Robot-Controllers)

- [:fontawesome-solid-graduation-cap:{ .lg .middle }
__Google Scholar__
](https://scholar.google.com/citations?view_op=view_citation&hl=en&user=5UOUU7MAAAAJ&sortby=pubdate&citft=1&email_for_op=jasonayoder%40gmail.com&citation_for_view=5UOUU7MAAAAJ:BqipwSGYUEgC)

</div>

???+ abstract
    This paper represents the first use of a field programmable transistor array (FPTA) to develop robot control circuits through direct evolution in analog hardware. The team successfully created controllers for a physical robot capable of executing multiple visually-guided tasks, including locating targets within complex settings. The system also evolved circuits for speech recognition, enabling voice-activated behavioral switching. A notable innovation was the coevolution of visual features alongside controllers, accomplishing simultaneous dimensionality reduction, feature extraction, and selection. The work demonstrates that intrinsic analog evolution remains a viable approach for producing sophisticated robot controllers.

??? quote "Suggested Citation"
    Garvie, M., Flascher, I., Philippides, A., Thompson, A., & Husbands, P. (2020). Evolved transistor array robot controllers. *Evolutionary Computation*, 28(4), 677–708. MIT Press.
