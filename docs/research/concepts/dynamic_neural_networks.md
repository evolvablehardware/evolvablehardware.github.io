# Dynamical Neural Networks

Dynamical neural networks, often implemented as Continuous-Time Recurrent Neural Networks (CTRNNs), are a powerful way of modeling behavior that unfolds over time. Unlike standard neural networks that process information in fixed steps, CTRNNs operate as smooth, continuous systems whose internal activity evolves moment by moment. They’re not just computing outputs — they’re generating dynamics, much like a biological neuron whose electrical state changes continuously in response to inputs.

At the heart of a CTRNN is a simple idea: neurons have internal states that drift, settle, or oscillate based on the signals they receive. These states feed back into the network, creating rich loops of interaction. Because of this, CTRNNs can produce memory, rhythms, coordinated behaviors, and subtle responses to environmental changes — all emerging naturally from the network’s dynamics. This makes them ideal for studying embodied agents, adaptive controllers, and systems that must operate in the real world rather than in discrete, scripted steps.

For our lab, CTRNNs are vital because they blend biology, computation, and continuous-time physics in a way that aligns with evolvable hardware. Their smooth, ongoing activity maps naturally onto analog-style behaviors that FPGAs and reconfigurable circuits can express. When evolved directly on hardware, CTRNNs can generate lifelike behaviors, navigate noisy environments, and adapt in real time. By exploring dynamical neural networks at the circuit level, we aim to capture a deeper kind of intelligence — one driven by flows of activity rather than static instructions, and one that can evolve to meet the demands of changing worlds.
