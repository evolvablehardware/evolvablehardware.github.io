# Core Concepts in Evolvable Hardware

Evolvable hardware is a new approach to designing computing systems that can adapt, improve, and sometimes even surprise us. Instead of treating hardware as a fixed, unchanging machine, evolvable hardware allows circuits to evolve directly on [Reconfigurable Hardware](reconfigurable_hardware.md) such as Field-Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs). Guided by principles from [Evolutionary Computation](evolutionary_computation.md), these systems generate diverse designs, test them in real time, and refine their behavior based on performance — much like nature shapes organisms through generations of adaptation.

This vision is deeply influenced by ideas from across biology and computing. Concepts from [Biologically Inspired Computing](bio_inspired_computing.md) help us see hardware not as static logic, but as dynamic, adaptive processes to be shaped by interaction and survival. Through [Reconfigurable Hardware](reconfigurable_hardware.md) platforms like FPGAs, we can rewrite circuitry on the fly, opening the door for evolution to create novel solutions that traditional engineering would rarely imagine. Techniques from [Approximate Computing](approximate_computing.md) allow evolved circuits to trade precision for speed, efficiency, and robustness — often discovering clever shortcuts that leverage physical properties of the substrate.

Together, these perspectives position evolvable hardware as a pathway toward technologies that don’t just compute — they adapt, interact, and continually grow more capable over time. They also connect with other [advanced concepts](advanced_concepts.md) that are reshaping our understanding of intelligence and computation. By exploring these core ideas, we can better understand the potential of evolvable hardware to revolutionize how we design and interact with technology in the future.
