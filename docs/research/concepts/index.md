# Core Concepts in Evolvable Hardware

Evolvable hardware is a new approach to designing computing systems that can adapt, improve, and sometimes even surprise us. Instead of treating hardware as a fixed, unchanging machine, evolvable hardware allows circuits to evolve directly on [Reconfigurable Hardware](reconfigurable_hardware.md) such as Field-Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs). Guided by principles from [Evolutionary Computation](evolutionary_computation.md), these systems generate diverse designs, test them in real time, and refine their behavior based on performance — much like nature shapes organisms through generations of adaptation.

This vision is deeply influenced by ideas from across biology and computing. Concepts from [Biologically Inspired Computing](bio_inspired_computing.md) help us see hardware not as static logic, but as dynamic, adaptive processes shaped by interaction and survival. Through [Reconfigurable Hardware](reconfigurable_hardware.md) platforms like FPGAs, we can rewrite circuitry on the fly, opening the door for evolution to create novel solutions that traditional engineering would rarely imagine. These solutions may operate under real-world constraints, embracing the resilience-oriented mindset of [Mortal Computation](mortal_computation.md), where systems must function even as parts fail or conditions change.

Evolvable hardware also intersects with emerging models of intelligence and computation. [Neuromorphic Hardware](neuromorphic_hardware.md) and [Dynamical Neural Networks](dynamic_neural_networks.md) show how lifelike behavior can emerge from continuous, interconnected activity instead of rigid, sequential instructions. Techniques from [Approximate Computing](approximate_computing.md) allow evolved circuits to trade precision for speed, efficiency, and robustness — often discovering clever shortcuts that leverage physical properties of the substrate. And ideas from [Embodied Intelligence](embodied_intelligence.md) remind us that the smartest behaviors often arise from tight coupling between body, environment, and control, which evolved hardware naturally exploits as it learns directly in the physical world.

Together, these perspectives position evolvable hardware as a pathway toward technologies that don’t just compute — they adapt, interact, and continually grow more capable over time.
